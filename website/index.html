How it works

  1. Signed with a passkey

    Someone signs

    A person, or someone who may sign for a company, says what one agent may do: for whom, toward whom, how much, until when. They sign with a passkey.

  2. getgleipnir.com/link/v/#eyJh…Code 4821

    The agent carries it

    The mandate travels with the agent as a link, a code or a protocol field. It works outside the app that issued it.

    The other side checks

    Before acting, the bank or call centre checks the signature, the scope and whether it was revoked. It uses its own records to decide if the signer counts.

    Everyone keeps the proof

    A wrong request stops or goes back to the person. When a dispute comes months later, the signed record answers it.

Questions

What is Gleipnir?

A signed mandate for AI agents. A person or company states what one agent may do, and the other side can check that before acting.

Do you move money or see our data?

No. Your bank sends payments as usual. The checks can run on your own systems, and the mandate link page runs in the browser.

Does it stop an agent from making mistakes?

No. It proves who allowed what. A fake invoice from an approved supplier, or a bad decision inside the allowed scope, is not something a mandate can catch.

Does it prove who the signer is?

Only if the checker knows the key. A bank uses its own records of who may sign for a company. A mandate link on its own proves that the holder of a key signed this scope, nothing more.

Is it legally binding?

We don’t know yet, and it will differ by country and by what the agent does. Ask your counsel before relying on it.

Don’t Visa and Mastercard do this?

For card payments, yes. Limits inside one app also exist. A mandate is meant to be checked by anyone, across providers and outside payments.

Can I use it today?

The specification, the Python and TypeScript checkers, the MCP server and the demos are open source under Apache 2.0. We have no production customers yet.
