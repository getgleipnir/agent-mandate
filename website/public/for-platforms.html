Beyond payments

Spec 0.6 adds a profile for agent protocols: the agent is bound by its key, counterparties are domains, no personal data travels, and a person can approve one operation with a passkey. It is a draft written around payments. Other actions are open work, and we want to do them with real teams.

For agents that call or write to companies today, try the experimental mandate link.

What you get

  • A power of attorney per agent, signed by a person who may sign for your customer’s company: approved payees, limits per payment and per period, accounts, expiry.
  • One check before every transfer, run by you or by your partner bank, with the same result everywhere.
  • Signed decisions you can hand to your partner bank or an auditor: who authorised the payment, and what was checked.
  • Revocation that works at once: your customer switches an agent off, and the next payment is blocked.

Why not just limits inside the platform?

Limits and approval flows inside your product are a good start, and they stay. They live in your database, so they mean little to a bank that executes the transfer or to a customer who banks elsewhere. A power of attorney signed by the customer’s signatory is evidence any bank can check against its own records, in the open format.

For the agents: an MCP server

The agent side ships as an MCP server with four tools:

  • mandate_scope: what the agent may pay right now.
  • request_mandate: ask a signatory for a power of attorney.
  • attach_mandate: sign the payment and attach the mandate.
  • check_payment: ask the bank’s checker before sending.

A tricked agent, for example one following a hidden instruction in an invoice, gets a clear block and a reason to show the person it works for, before any money moves.

Start with a shadow pilot

Run the checker next to your existing flow for four weeks, without blocking anything. Questions

Does this change how our payments are executed?

No. Transfers go through your bank partners as today. The check adds a decision before execution.

Can we run the checker ourselves?

Yes. The reference checker and the MCP server are open source (Apache 2.0).
