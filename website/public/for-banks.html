Banks and payment service providers

For banks and PSPs: know the agent before you send

AI agents are starting to send SEPA, ACH and wire transfers through your APIs. Gleipnir gives you one extra check before execution: was this agent authorised by someone who may sign for the company, for this payee and this amount?

POST /check

{ "mandate": { … signed by K. Berger },

  "payment": { "amount": "18400.00",

    "payee": "Brenner Stahl AG" } }

200 { "outcome": "allow",

  "decision": { … signed by the bank } }

Open-source reference checker. You can run it today.

Why now

Federal Reserve Governor Waller named “proving that an agent has the authority to pay on the buyer’s behalf” as a question for payments (Sibos 2026). €2.2bn was lost to credit transfer fraud in the EEA in 2024 (EBA and ECB). A mandate doesn’t stop all of that. It stops an agent paying someone the company never approved.

What you check

  • Signatory authority, from your own records. The power of attorney counts only if it is signed by a person your KYB says may sign for the company, with a key you enrolled for them. Trust never comes from the mandate itself.
  • Your customer’s account. The debtor account is one you hold for that company.
  • The agent. The payment is signed by the agent key the mandate names.
  • Scope. Rail, currency, approved payees, per-payment and per-period limits.
  • Still valid. Validity window, revocations, optional signed status.

Ordered checks, each with a reason code. The checker uses its own clock, never one the agent supplies, and it fails closed: a dependency it cannot reach gives escalate, never allow.

What you get back

  • allow: execute. block: do not execute, with the reason. escalate: a person decides, for example over the limit, and signs an override.
  • A decision signed with your key, verifiable offline years later: who authorised the payment, what was checked, when.
  • Evidence of the payer’s authorisation for disputes and audits, at the point where PSD2 asks whether the payer consented.

How it fits your stack

  • A reference checker you run yourself: HTTP POST /check, /revoke and /keys. No call to Gleipnir is needed to check a payment.
  • Your KYB stays the source of truth for who may sign. Company registers, LEI and vLEI plug in as optional sources.
  • ISO 20022: the checked payment maps to pain.001.001.09, with the mandate, payment and decision hashes in SplmtryData.
  • Your checker keys are published at /.well-known/gleipnir-checker.json with validity windows.
  • We never move money, hold funds or see your customers’ data unless you send it to us.

Status

Open specification 0.6, Python and TypeScript reference checkers with shared test vectors, and an MCP server for agents, all on GitHub under Apache 2.0. We are looking for one or two banks or PSPs to run a pilot on outgoing agent transfers, first in shadow mode: you see what the check would have decided, without blocking anything.

Questions

Does Gleipnir replace our KYB?

No. Your KYB is where signatory authority comes from. Gleipnir checks the agent's power of attorney against it.

Do payments go through Gleipnir?

No. You execute transfers as today. The check runs in your environment before execution.

What happens if the checker cannot reach a system it needs?

It returns escalate, never allow. A payment it could not fully check is never allowed.
