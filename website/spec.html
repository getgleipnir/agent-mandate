The mandate specification

Version 0.6, a draft. A person who may sign states what one agent may do, and the other side checks it and records a signed decision.

Objects

Mandatesigned by a person
Paymentsigned by the agent
Decisionsigned by the checker
Mandate
Company, agent, signers, accounts, payees, rails, limits and validity. Signed with a passkey (WebAuthn), Ed25519 or ECDSA P-256, alone or jointly.
Payment
The transfer the agent wants to make, bound to the mandate by hash and signed by the agent.
Decision
Allow, block or escalate, with reason codes, signed by the checker.
Revocation
Withdrawing a mandate, effective from the next check. Signed status lists and Token Status List.
Override
A person signs a decision on an escalated payment.
Delegation
Authority passed down a chain, never wider than its source.
Operation approval
A person’s signature over one revision of one operation, for agent protocols (0.6).

Where trust comes from

Only from the checker’s own records, such as a bank’s KYB and enrolled keys, or a vLEI credential. Never from the mandate itself. After that, every link is a signature.

Scope of this version

0.6 is written for company payments, with a profile for agent protocols in section 14. Use by consumers and for actions other than payments is open work. The experimental mandate link is a separate prototype, outside this spec.

Encoding and interoperability

RFC 8785 canonical JSON, SHA-256, base64url without padding, strict RFC 3339 timestamps, closed object shapes. ISO 20022 pain.001 mapping. RFC 7638 key thumbprints in the agent profile.

A Python reference and a TypeScript port pass the same published test vectors and verify each other’s decisions. Licence: Apache 2.0.
